Kitchen Water Damage Help in Rocky Mount

A kitchen has more water connections than any other room: the faucet, the drain, the disposal, the dishwasher and the refrigerator line. Almost every one sits out of view, behind an appliance or inside a cabinet. When one drips, the water runs to the back of the cabinet base and spreads under the floor. For homeowners in Rocky Mount, our job is to track it down, dry it out, and preserve all of the kitchen that can be saved.

In a wet kitchen, the cabinets are where the real expense lies. Plywood and solid wood will often dry successfully, whereas a particleboard box puffs up quickly and never returns to size. Toe kicks come off and a few small, concealed holes are drilled so dry air can travel behind and below the cabinets, which lets cabinets and countertops stay put in many kitchens during drying.

Warning Signs

  • Side panels on the base cabinets that are flaking or swollen at the bottom
  • Paint that bubbles, or a stain, on the wall at the back of the sink or dishwasher
  • Stale, damp air that hits you when the doors under the sink are opened
  • Cabinet floor under the sink that is warped, stained, or soft to the touch
  • A water bill that climbs, or a faint hiss or drip sound, with every tap turned off
  • Swollen or darkened toe kicks, or ones separating from the base of the cabinets
If Nothing Is Done

What Happens if You Wait

First hour

Water Runs Under

A failed fitting or hose sends water under appliances and to the rear of the cabinets. It slips beneath flooring through the seams and edges.

24 to 48 hours

Cabinets Start Swelling

Toe kicks and particleboard cabinet bases absorb the water and swell. In the dark, enclosed gap below the cabinets, and at the back of the dishwasher, mold can start.

Days 3 through 7

Floors Begin Moving

As adhesive releases, vinyl begins lifting, while hardwood boards cup and laminate edges swell. The doors and drawers on the cabinets go out of alignment.

After a week

Removal Becomes Likely

A cabinet box that has swollen is not coming back. Subfloor may be delaminating, and mold behind the cabinets often means removing them along with the countertop above.

Our Process

How We Handle Kitchen Water Damage

1

Stop the Source

The fixture or appliance line that leaked is shut off, the refrigerator or dishwasher is pulled if necessary, and we confirm the failed connection so it gets repaired before we start drying.

2

Map the Moisture

Using moisture meters and thermal imaging, we check cabinet bases, the wall behind them, and the flooring across the room. Under kitchen flooring, water often goes farther than anyone expects.

3

Dry Without Demolition

With the toe kicks off, air gets pushed below the cabinets and behind them. For several days the dehumidifiers and air movers keep running, and damp cavities receive an antimicrobial treatment.

4

Remove Only Damage

We carefully remove any flooring, subfloor or cabinet section that cannot be saved. We confirm dry readings, then reinstall appliances and help you line up any repairs that remain.

Hidden Risks

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Flooring Over Flooring

Many kitchens have a newer floor installed on top of an older one. Trapped water sits between the two, with no way to evaporate and no sign on the surface.

The Ice Maker Line

The thin tube feeding the ice maker can leak a few drops a minute. Behind a fridge that never moves, that is enough to rot flooring and the wall behind.

Under the Base Cabinets

Base cabinets sit over a gap that is boxed in all around. Water collects there, stays for weeks, and grows mold that nobody sees until the cabinets are pulled.

The Wall Behind the Cabinets

Behind the dishwasher and sink, the wall carries both the drain and the supply lines. Cabinets fully cover that drywall and framing, so a leak can soak them and mold can spread out of sight.
