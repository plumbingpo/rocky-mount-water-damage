Few emergencies in a building are as hazardous as sewage backing up into it. Raw sewage contains dangerous bacteria, viruses, parasites and other pathogens, including E. coli, hepatitis, salmonella and giardia, that pose serious health risks to anyone who touches contaminated materials. When it happens in Rocky Mount, Rocky Mount Restoration is available 24/7 with technicians trained for this work, proper protective gear and disinfectants registered with the EPA, everything needed for a safe decontamination.

Water damage comes in categories, and sewage sits in the worst one: Category 3, called black water, the highest level of contamination and danger. Because of it, cleanup follows the toughest rules in the trade, which means full protective gear, barriers to contain the area, tearing out any porous material that met sewage and disinfecting all that is left. We work to the IICRC S500 standard that governs Category 3 losses, with the goal of returning your property to a condition safe to occupy.

The cleanup process involves removing all standing sewage, extracting contaminated water from structural materials, removing and properly disposing of every porous material that contacted sewage (carpet, padding, drywall, insulation), thoroughly disinfecting hard surfaces, applying antimicrobial treatments and completing structural drying to prevent secondary mold growth. We also deodorize the affected area with industrial-grade hydroxyl generators and thermal fogging equipment to eliminate lingering sewage odors.

Maintenance Tips for Sewage Cleanup

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • A drain that is slowing down should be dealt with early, while it is still partly open
  • Roots from trees close to the sewer line work their way into pipes and block them, so have them cleared
  • Hair and other debris are easy to stop with simple strainers on tub, shower and sink drains
  • Toilet paper is the only thing to flush, and that rules out wipes sold as flushable, which still clog
  • A backwater valve at the sewer connection stops the city system from backing up into your home
  • Have your sewer line inspected with a camera every 2 to 3 years to catch potential problems

Why Sewage Cleanup Is Not a DIY Job

No homeowner should try to clean up sewage personally. Because raw sewage is a biological hazard, the work demands trained people, protective equipment (gloves, boots, respirators and Tyvek suits) and disinfection products registered with the EPA and not sold to the public. Exposure to sewage pathogens can cause serious illness, including gastrointestinal disease, hepatitis, respiratory infections and skin infections.

Disposal matters as well, since everything the sewage contaminated has to be handled as biohazard waste. Putting carpet, insulation or drywall soaked with sewage out with household trash breaks the law and endangers sanitation workers and your community. A certified restoration company follows proper disposal protocols and works with licensed facilities that accept biohazard waste.

Signs It Is Time for Sewage Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Foul-smelling moisture showing up on flooring or along the walls
  • Drain openings or basement slabs coated with visible sewage residue
  • Running a sink or washer and hearing a toilet or nearby drain gurgle
  • Every sink, tub and shower draining slowly even after you plunge them
  • Family members falling ill, especially with gastrointestinal trouble, for no obvious reason
  • A toilet overflow containing waste that has spread onto flooring or carpet
  • A cleanout that weeps sewage, or wet foul patches in the lawn along the sewer path
