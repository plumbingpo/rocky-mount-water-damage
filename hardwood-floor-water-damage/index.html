Hardwood Floor Water Damage in Rocky Mount

Hardwood responds fast when it gets wet. Boards take on moisture from below and from the edges, swell against each other and start to cup, with the edges rising higher than the center. A floor in this condition is not necessarily ruined. When drying starts within the first few days, solid hardwood frequently lies flat again. The key is pulling moisture out of the boards and the subfloor underneath at a steady, controlled pace.

We dry hardwood with floor drying mats. Each mat is sealed against the floor and hooked to a vacuum unit, which pulls moisture upward through seams and boards as dehumidifiers dry the air in the room. This takes longer than drying carpet or drywall, often a week or more. Sanding has to wait until the wood is fully dry. Sand a cupped floor too soon and it will crown later.

What It Looks Like

  • Dark gray or black stains along seams and around nail heads
  • Crowning, meaning the middle of each board bulges above its edges
  • Gaps opening between boards after a floor has dried too fast
  • Cupping, where each board's edges rise above its middle
  • Fasteners working loose, heard as squeaks where the floor was soaked
  • Flooring that gives under your weight in the area that got wet
Our Process

How We Handle Hardwood Floor Water Damage

1

Measure the Moisture

We take readings on the boards, the subfloor and unaffected flooring nearby for comparison. The comparison shows how far down moisture reached and how likely the floor is to recover.

2

Extract Standing Water

Standing water is extracted right away and rugs and furniture are moved off the wet area. Boards swell less when the seams stop taking in water sooner.

3

Set Drying Mats

A vacuum system hooked to mats sealed on the surface lifts moisture out of the boards from above. Dehumidifiers control the room so the wood dries evenly without cracking.

4

Monitor and Advise

Readings are logged every one to two days and the equipment stays until the wet zone equals the dry comparison areas. At that point you hear from us if it needs nothing, some replacement boards, or a refinish.

Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

The first few hours

Seepage Below the Boards

Seams let the water through, and it fans out in the layer between flooring and subfloor. You can towel off the top and still have a saturated underside.

Hours 24 to 48

Edges Begin to Rise

The edges come up as each board expands. You might notice haze in the topcoat. Mold can begin on the subfloor and the unfinished underside of the wood.

Day 3 to 7

Boards Start Lifting

Swelling builds pressure across the floor. Boards crush against each other, fasteners loosen and sections can begin to lift off the subfloor.

Past one week

Beyond Drying

Expect a good chance of mold below, dark staining and boards that have buckled. At this stage most of the wet area needs to be replaced rather than dried.

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Refinishing Before It Is Dry

Sanding cupped boards flat while they are still wet removes the raised edges. When the wood finishes drying, the centers end up high and the floor is crowned.

Stains From Nails and Staples

Lingering moisture rusts the fasteners, which then hold the boards less firmly. The rust also bleeds into the wood as dark stains that sanding may not remove.

Growth Beneath the Floor

No air moves and no light reaches the gap between the hardwood and the subfloor. Growth there is invisible from above and shows up first as a musty smell.

Water That Keeps Moving

Moisture beneath flooring does not stay put. Next door rooms, the area under cabinets and walls, and the ceiling below are all places it can end up.
