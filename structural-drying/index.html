Of all the stages in restoring water damage, drying the structure counts most, and it is the stage most often botched by people without training. After extraction removes the standing water, significant moisture remains trapped in structural materials, including drywall, wood framing, subflooring, concrete, insulation and other building components. Controlled evaporation and dehumidification, applied scientifically, are needed to get that moisture out before a wet structure develops mold, rotting wood, degraded materials and odors.

The structural drying technicians at Rocky Mount Restoration are IICRC-certified and trained in psychrometry, the science of how temperature, humidity and moisture in air relate. For each job they apply it to calculate airflow patterns, temperature settings and the placement of equipment that will give the best conditions for evaporation. Because hardwood, drywall, plaster and concrete do not dry at the same speed, the method has to change with the material to bring it to acceptable moisture content without harming it further.

Every day of the drying process, we check moisture using thermal imaging cameras, thermo-hygrometers and calibrated moisture meters. The result is a detailed log of readings that tracks how moisture comes down over time in each material that got wet. Each day we use those numbers to reposition or retune the equipment so drying stays efficient. We call a job dry when every affected material meets the moisture standard in the IICRC S500 guidelines, and never just because a surface feels dry.

Structural Drying: What Homeowners Ask

A drying log is the record of moisture readings we take throughout the job. You can see progressive moisture reduction for every material that was wet. When everything reaches dry standard, the closing readings go into the log as well. The complete log is available for your insurance claim and your own records.
Our technicians take readings from all the affected materials using calibrated moisture meters. Drying is complete when readings fall within acceptable ranges established by IICRC S500 standards and compared to dry reference materials in unaffected areas. Touch and appearance by themselves are not proof of anything.
Most of the time there is no need to leave while the structure dries. The equipment is safe but noisy. We can discuss strategies to minimize disruption, such as positioning equipment away from bedrooms or adjusting airflow patterns. Please leave the equipment running while you are home.
Relying on a few fans is the leading reason secondary mold shows up after water damage. Our LGR dehumidifiers take 10 to 20 times as much moisture out of the air as a home model. We pair them with high-velocity air movers that direct airflow exactly where surfaces need to evaporate. And unless you measure with a meter, you cannot tell when the job is done.
Most residential structural drying projects take 3 to 5 days. Building construction, ambient conditions, the materials involved and the degree of saturation all change how long it takes. Heavily saturated structures and commercial jobs can need 5 to 7 days, sometimes longer. Daily monitoring lets us keep you informed about the timeline.

Why Structural Drying Is Not a DIY Job

Structural drying is a science, not a guessing game. Secondary mold damage most often traces back to someone who put down fans and hoped things would dry. To do it professionally, you have to know how each material takes on and gives up moisture, work out the grain depression, meaning the moisture removal capacity, for the job, and check progress every day. Get it wrong in one direction and mold feeds on leftover moisture, get it wrong in the other and wood or similar materials are damaged by over-drying.

Consumer products and the equipment professionals dry structures with have very little in common. Commercial LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers remove 10 to 20 times more moisture from the air than residential dehumidifiers, and high-velocity air movers create the precise airflow patterns needed to accelerate evaporation from structural surfaces. Machines like these cost a great deal and take training to use well, which explains why professional results are so much better than a do-it-yourself effort.

Structural Drying Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Keep belongings off the machines and out of the path of the air movers
  • Each unit is positioned according to the moisture map, so ask before shifting anything
  • Moisture can sit deep in a material whose surface feels dry, so rely on the meter readings
  • The dehumidifiers and air movers should run around the clock until our technician takes them out
  • Do not turn off professional drying equipment to reduce noise, because every hour matters
  • Dehumidifiers lose efficiency when outside air gets in, so keep drying areas closed up

Signs It Is Time for Structural Drying

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Drywall that is soft, swollen or shows moisture staining
  • Paint or wallpaper that bubbles, peels or blisters after a leak
  • After water damage, a meter shows elevated moisture in the floor, wall or ceiling
  • A stale, musty smell that lingers even though the water was extracted
  • Humidity that will not come down in the affected space, whatever ventilation you try
  • Swollen trim and baseboards separating at the seams
  • Earlier water damage that was never professionally dried and now shows deterioration
